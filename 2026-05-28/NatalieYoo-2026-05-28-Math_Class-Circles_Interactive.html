Tap a word, watch it glow

Every circle is built from these pieces. The word circle comes from Latin circulus — "a little ring."

Center Radius Diameter Chord Arc
Tap any word above. Radius = center to the edge. Diameter = all the way across through the center (it is $2r$). A chord touches the edge twice but skips the center. An arc is a curved slice of the edge.

The number hiding in every circle

In class we measured "all the way around" (C) and "across" (D) for real objects, then divided. Slide to try different circles — the ratio barely moves.

C ÷ D always lands on  3.14
No matter how big the circle is, $\dfrac{C}{D}$ is always the same special number: $\pi \approx 3.14$. So $C = \pi D = 2\pi r$.

Euclid's slice-and-rearrange

Cut the circle into skinny wedges, then lay them in a zig-zag row. The more slices, the flatter the bottom — it turns into a rectangle!

The rearranged shape is almost a rectangle. Its height is the radius $r$, and its width is half the way around, $\pi r$. So the area is height × width $= r \times \pi r = \pi r^2$. That is where $A=\pi r^2$ comes from!
